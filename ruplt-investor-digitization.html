6. Investor assessment · energy security & AI infrastructure

01 · Demand confirmation is the gating factor

RUPTL uses customer confidence for large high-voltage loads: contracts, connection payments and grid construction are stronger evidence than MoUs. Apply this hierarchy to AI data-center announcements before treating them as firm PLN load.

Source: PDF pp.46, 53.

02 · Site-level reliability is not a system-wide MW number

N-1 transmission and substation contingency planning is required, but this document does not prove N-1 availability for any AI campus. Request feeder, transformer, protection and energization studies.

Source: PDF pp.89–90.

03 · Grid investment can precede peak-load bottlenecks

The RUPTL describes a typical 70–80% GI loading range for planning transformer reinforcement, subject to N-1 exceptions. This is a planning criterion, not evidence that Bekasi or Batang substations are currently near saturation.

Source: PDF p.90.

04 · Reservoir solar is optionality, not bankable supply

Its 109 entries amount to around 13.9 GW of indicative area-based potential. The duplicate Gembong entry, trans-provincial Karangnongko and lack of procurement or grid-connection records prevent interpreting the sum as an executable PV investment portfolio.

Source: Table 2.2, PDF pp.82–83.

05 · BESS investment needs duration and location

Nationally planned storage power of 10.256 GW does not specify hours of storage, deliverable energy in GWh, dispatch profile or which local data-center clusters benefit. BESS opportunities therefore require a separately verified regional siting and grid-services model.

Source: decree p.5; storage strategy pp.74–76.

06 · Carbon claims require energy, not nameplate

The 2034 EBT target of 34.3% concerns electricity generation mix. The 42.569 GW EBT buildout is installed-capacity planning; neither proves renewable power actually contracted or delivered hourly to a data center.

Source: decree p.5.

07 · Annexes are essential for location intelligence

The uploaded 100 pages name annex A4 (Kepulauan Riau), B3 (Jawa Barat) and B4 (Jawa Tengah), but do not contain their project-level schedules. No exact GI COD, additional MW or investor ranking should be inferred for these locations yet.

Source: PDF p.27.

08 · Smart-meter data requires reconciliation

AMI implementation is an important operational technology signal, but the printed post-2033 roll-out series and the 2037 accompanying prose conflict. Any investor dashboard should show the contradiction rather than blending it into a verified adoption trend.

Source: PDF pp.98–99.
